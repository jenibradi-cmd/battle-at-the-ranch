# STILLWATER — The Master Buying, Merchandising, Sourcing & Product-Development Strategy

*A founder-level operating document. Written from the combined seat of a global retail strategist, merchant, lifestyle-brand founder, luxury-hotel curator, anthropologist, consumer psychologist, sourcing expert, product developer, and trend forecaster.*

> **Positioning thesis.** Stillwater is not a home store with a spa line bolted on. Stillwater sells **time, ritual, and belonging** — the felt experience of a slow life — and uses product as the membrane between the customer and that feeling. Every SKU must answer one question: *"Does this make an ordinary moment feel consecrated?"* If it doesn't, it doesn't belong.

The brand reference point is the intersection of **Toast's restraint**, **Flamingo Estate's narrative ingredient theater**, **Blackberry Farm's estate-as-product**, **John Derian's curation-as-authorship**, and **RH's gravitational real estate**. Stillwater's unfair advantage is **ritual** — a layer none of the named competitors fully own.

---

## How to read this document

- **Margin targets** are stated as gross margin (GM%) at retail, before occupancy/labor.
- **Keystone** = 2.0x cost → 50% GM. **Triple keystone** = 3.0x → ~67% GM.
- **MOQ** = minimum order quantity. **LT** = lead time. **FOB** = price at factory/port of origin.
- Sourcing names are real clusters and representative makers/agents, given as *starting points for discovery*, not endorsements or guarantees of current capacity.

# PART 1 — CUSTOMER PSYCHOLOGY

### The Stillwater customer, anthropologically

Primary: **women 38–62**, household income $180k+, often a second home or an aspiration to one. She is "**time-affluent-seeking**" — she has money but feels starved of unhurried time, and she buys objects that *promise to slow time down*. Secondary: **the gifter** (any gender, buying up), and **the host** (identity built around gathering people).

Her core psychological tension: she wants to feel **deep, rooted, and intentional** in a culture that feels disposable and accelerated. She is post-status in the logo sense but intensely status-aware in the *taste* sense. Her flex is **discernment**, not price.

Three sentences govern every category below:
- **What customers SAY** (stated, socially acceptable).
- **What they MEAN** (the emotional truth under it).
- **What they SECRETLY WISH existed** (the unmet opportunity = your roadmap).

---

### 1. Spa Essentials (bath salts, oils, candles, soaps, body care, fragrance, room/linen sprays)

- **Stated needs:** "I want to relax / something that smells good / a nicer soap than the grocery store."
- **Stated wants:** clean ingredients, beautiful packaging, a scent that's "not too perfumey."
- **Hidden wants:** permission to stop; a sensory signal that the workday is *over*; a bathroom that looks like a hotel she once loved.
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Help me transition from output-mode to human-mode."* The bath is a threshold ritual, not hygiene.
- **Identity signals:** "I'm a person who takes care of herself." Owning Aesop/Diptyque-tier products = membership in the discerning class.
- **Status signals:** apothecary aesthetic on an open shelf (it's *meant to be seen*); refillable glass; named perfumers.
- **Belonging signals:** "everyone in my world burns this candle / uses this hand wash."
- **Luxury signals:** weight of the vessel, restraint of the label, ingredient provenance ("Calabrian bergamot"), and *negative space* on packaging.
- **Spiritual signals:** scent as memory/anointing; the candle as a secular votive.
- **Unmet opportunity:** **ritual sequencing** — products designed as a *choreography* (step 1 → 2 → 3) rather than a shelf of unrelated SKUs. Nobody owns "the 8-minute bath ceremony."

> **Say:** "I just want a nice candle." **Mean:** "I want my home to feel like the hotel where I finally exhaled." **Secretly wish existed:** a candle whose scent *changes* through the burn to mark the passage of an evening (top → heart → base as a designed arc).

### 2. Ritual Products (spa-in-a-box, morning/evening/Sabbath/hospitality/grief/celebration/seasonal)

- **Stated needs:** "a thoughtful gift," "something to help me start the day right."
- **Stated wants:** a curated set, an instruction card, a beautiful box.
- **Hidden wants:** a *script* for emotionally hard moments (grief, transition) where she doesn't know what to do or say.
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Give me a container for feelings I can't organize myself."* Ritual outsources emotional labor into objects + sequence.
- **Identity signals:** "I'm intentional / I have practices / I'm spiritually literate without being preachy."
- **Status signals:** giving a grief ritual box signals emotional sophistication — the rarest social flex.
- **Belonging signals:** shared liturgy ("we do the Sunday reset," "we light the Sabbath candle").
- **Luxury signals:** the *card* — the writing, the paper, the weight of the words.
- **Spiritual signals:** the entire category IS the spiritual signal. This is Stillwater's deepest moat.
- **Unmet opportunity:** **the grief ritual market is essentially unserved at this tier.** Sympathy = flowers + a basket. Nobody has built a beautiful, non-denominational, deeply considered grief object/ritual. This alone could define the brand.

> **Say:** "I need a hostess gift." **Mean:** "I want to be remembered as the most thoughtful person at the table." **Secretly wish existed:** a ritual she can *repeat* — not a one-time gift but a practice that becomes part of her identity (and a reorder for you).

### 3. Home Goods (tabletop, kitchen, linens, serving, decorative, storage, baskets)

- **Stated needs:** "plates / linens / something to organize."
- **Stated wants:** handmade look, neutral palette, "pieces that go together but aren't matchy."
- **Hidden wants:** to host a table that makes guests gasp quietly; to feel like the home is *finished* (it never is — that's the engine).
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Make my gatherings feel like an event and my daily meals feel sacred."*
- **Identity signals:** the handmade, the imperfect glaze, the linen that wrinkles "correctly."
- **Status signals:** knowing the maker's name; "it's East Fork / Heath / a small potter in Puglia."
- **Belonging signals:** the table as the altar of her social world.
- **Luxury signals:** mismatched-on-purpose; patina; provenance; the story of the hands.
- **Spiritual signals:** the table as communion; bread, salt, water as primal objects.
- **Unmet opportunity:** **the "complete table" sold as a curated scene**, not 40 SKUs to assemble. Decision fatigue is the enemy; curation is the product.

> **Say:** "I love handmade ceramics." **Mean:** "I want the warmth of imperfection without the risk of looking unintentional." **Secretly wish existed:** a tablescape she can buy *as a finished composition* and trust completely.

### 4. Antiques (European, English, French, primitive American, architectural salvage)

- **Stated needs:** "a unique piece," "something with soul / history."
- **Stated wants:** patina, one-of-a-kind, "a story."
- **Hidden wants:** to telegraph *old money / inherited taste* even when self-made; to anchor a too-new house in time.
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Give my home a soul and a lineage I can't buy new."*
- **Identity signals:** "I have an eye." Antiques are the ultimate taste credential.
- **Status signals:** the rarity, the dealer relationship, "I found it in Provence."
- **Belonging signals:** part of the dealer/collector tribe.
- **Luxury signals:** irreplaceability; the impossibility of duplication.
- **Spiritual signals:** objects that have witnessed other lives; *memento mori* warmth.
- **Unmet opportunity:** antiques **demystified and de-intimidated** for the aspirational buyer — curated, authenticated, styled, with confident provenance storytelling. RH does scale; nobody does *warm, accessible, storied curation* at $200–$2,000.

> **Say:** "I want a one-of-a-kind piece." **Mean:** "I want to look like I inherited taste, not bought it." **Secretly wish existed:** a trusted curator who tells her exactly which old thing is *right* so she can't get it wrong.

### 5. Garden (tools, seeds, harvesting, aprons, baskets, outdoor entertaining)

- **Stated needs:** "good tools," "pretty seed packets."
- **Stated wants:** heirloom varieties, French/Japanese tools, a linen apron.
- **Hidden wants:** to *perform* the slow life (the garden is the most Instagrammable virtue); to feel productive in a non-corporate way.
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Let me participate in something alive and seasonal."*
- **Identity signals:** the maker-gardener, the modern homesteader-aesthete.
- **Status signals:** the *time* gardening implies; heirloom Italian seeds; a Sneeboer or Japanese hori-hori.
- **Belonging signals:** the seasonal-living community.
- **Luxury signals:** forged steel, ash handles, brass, leather aprons that age.
- **Spiritual signals:** soil, cycles, harvest, gratitude — primal and biblical.
- **Unmet opportunity:** **garden-to-table ritual kits** and **heirloom seed collections curated by feeling/use** ("the cutting garden," "the Sabbath table garden") rather than by botanical SKU.

### 6. Jewelry (artisan silver, gemstone, symbolic, faith-inspired, everyday luxury)

- **Stated needs:** "something I can wear every day," "a meaningful piece."
- **Stated wants:** handmade, not flashy, "looks expensive but isn't logo."
- **Hidden wants:** a talisman; a piece that carries meaning/protection/faith without being literal or kitsch.
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Let me carry my values/faith/loved ones on my body."*
- **Identity signals:** the artisan maker; the symbol only the wearer fully understands.
- **Status signals:** quiet-luxury jewelry; recognizable-to-the-few materials (raw gemstone, oxidized silver, gold vermeil).
- **Belonging signals:** faith communities, mother/daughter pieces, friendship tokens.
- **Luxury signals:** weight, hand-finishing, the maker's mark.
- **Spiritual signals:** **enormous and underserved** — faith-inspired jewelry that is *beautiful* (not Christian-bookstore aesthetic) is a near-empty premium lane.
- **Unmet opportunity:** **symbolic/faith-inspired fine-ish jewelry at $80–$400 with genuine design credibility.** The gap between "tacky cross" and "$3,000 fine jewelry" is wide open.

### 7. Gifts (hostess, sympathy, birthday, milestone, corporate, seasonal)

- **Stated needs:** "I need a gift fast and it has to look thoughtful."
- **Stated wants:** ready-to-give, beautiful wrap, the right price-to-impressiveness ratio.
- **Hidden wants:** to be *seen as thoughtful* with minimal time invested; to never give a "wrong" gift.
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Make me look like I know exactly the right thing."*
- **Identity signals:** the giver of memorable gifts (a reputation she protects).
- **Status signals:** the wrap, the card, the "where did you find this?"
- **Belonging signals:** gifting as relationship maintenance.
- **Luxury signals:** packaging as the product; the unboxing.
- **Spiritual signals:** sympathy and milestone gifts = marking thresholds.
- **Unmet opportunity:** **corporate gifting at taste-level.** Companies overspend on soulless gifts. A Stillwater corporate program (curated, brandable subtly, ritual-driven) is high-margin, high-volume, recession-resilient B2B.

### 8. Apparel & Accessories (slow-fashion, natural fibers, artisan-made, timeless)

- **Stated needs:** "comfortable, beautiful, lasts."
- **Stated wants:** linen, organic cotton, no logos, "the uniform."
- **Hidden wants:** to look effortless and expensive; to opt out of trend cycles without looking frumpy.
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Dress me as the calm, rooted woman I'm becoming."*
- **Identity signals:** the Toast/Jenni Kayne aesthetic = "I've evolved past fast fashion."
- **Status signals:** quality only the wearer/initiated can read; provenance.
- **Belonging signals:** the slow-living wardrobe tribe.
- **Luxury signals:** fiber, drape, natural dye, repairability.
- **Spiritual signals:** simplicity as a value; dressing with intention.
- **Unmet opportunity:** **apparel as the loyalty flywheel for a home brand** (Jenni Kayne's model). Be cautious: apparel is operationally hard (size/returns). Enter via *accessories first* (the easy, high-margin, low-return entry — robes, wraps, scarves, aprons, slippers, totes).

### 9. Experience Products (workshops, subscriptions, membership, retreats, experiences)

- **Stated needs:** "I want to learn / do something / get away."
- **Stated wants:** a class, a box subscription, a retreat.
- **Hidden wants:** transformation, community, and *access* — to be an insider, not a customer.
- **Emotional JTBD:** *"Let me belong to something and become someone."*
- **Identity signals:** membership; "I did the Stillwater retreat."
- **Status signals:** scarcity, invitation, the waitlist.
- **Belonging signals:** the entire point — recurring community.
- **Luxury signals:** intimacy, small group size, named hosts, place.
- **Spiritual signals:** retreat = pilgrimage; subscription = practice.
- **Unmet opportunity:** **membership that converts customers into congregation.** Soho House for the slow-living woman. This is the LTV multiplier and the brand's long-term moat.

### Where they AGREE (the consensus playbook)
1. **Private label / exclusivity is the margin engine.** Every winner controls product nobody else can sell.
2. **Story is not marketing — it's the product spec.** Provenance, maker, place. Toast, Flamingo, Derian, RH all sell narrative first.
3. **Curation > assortment.** The *edit* is the value. They subtract relentlessly.
4. **Experience/place anchors retail.** RH restaurants, Terrain café, Soho House. Hospitality drives basket and dwell time.
5. **Hero products do disproportionate work.** A few icons define the brand and carry inventory depth; the long tail creates discovery.
6. **Founder/curator taste is the algorithm.** Magnolia, Derian, Amber, Goop — a human eye is the moat.

### Where they DIFFER (the strategic forks)
- **Wide-shallow vs. deep-hero.** Anthropologie/Derian = treasure hunt, constant churn. RH/Pottery Barn = dominate few, deep stock. → *Stillwater should run a hybrid: deep on private-label hero/consumables, wide-shallow on antiques/curated discovery.*
- **Vertical vs. curatorial.** RH/PB own the supply chain; Derian/Goop curate others' goods. → *Stillwater: vertical on consumables (spa/ritual/PL home), curatorial on antiques/artisan/jewelry.*
- **Volume-accessible vs. scarcity-premium.** PB/White Company = depth & replenishment. Derian/Flamingo = scarcity & premium. → *Stillwater leans premium-scarcity for brand goods, with a replenishable consumables base for cash flow.*
- **Trend-translation vs. timelessness.** Magnolia/Anthro chase freshness; Toast/Bunny Williams reject trend. → *Stillwater is firmly timeless* (trend is the enemy of "slow").

### Product testing methodology (synthesized best practice)
- **Drop/limited-edition as live test** (Goop, Flamingo, Magnolia): release small, read sell-through, reorder winners as core.
- **Email/insider pre-sell** to read demand before committing inventory.
- **Store/vignette A-B**: same product, two placements, read conversion.
- **Sell-through gates**: >70% sell-through at full price in 6–8 weeks → graduate to core; <40% → markdown and cut.
- **Gift-set bundling test**: does the SKU sell better solo or in a ritual set? (Almost always the set wins → bundle.)

# PART 3 — GLOBAL SOURCING MAP

> Philosophy (from the brief): **find lesser-known artisan clusters, not trade-show booths.** The trade-show vendor sells to everyone; the cluster gives you exclusivity and story. Below, each country lists region/city, specialty, indicative MOQ, pricing posture, lead time, trade-offs, and the *hidden* opportunity.

### PORTUGAL — the European value-quality sweet spot
- **Region/City:** Caldas da Rainha & Alcobaça (ceramics); São João da Madeira (footwear/felt); north (Guimarães/Barcelos) textiles & linen; Marinha Grande (glass); Fátima (devotional/religious goods).
- **Specialties:** hand-glazed stoneware, faience, linen/cotton textiles, cork, glassware, soap (Castelbel/Confiança heritage), devotional objects.
- **MOQ:** ceramics 100–300/shape; textiles 200–500m or 50–100 units; soap 500–1,000.
- **Pricing:** mid (FOB stoneware dinner plate ~$4–9; linen napkin ~$3–6). **LT:** 60–120 days.
- **Trade-offs:** EU labor cost > Asia but << France/Italy; capacity tightening as everyone discovers it.
- **Hidden opportunity:** **Fátima's religious-goods cluster** can produce *beautiful* faith jewelry/objects if redesigned away from kitsch — direct line to Stillwater's underserved faith category. Also small Caldas potteries for exclusive glazes.

### SPAIN
- **Region/City:** Valencia/La Alcora & Manises (ceramics, tile); Ubrique (Andalusia, leather — makes for major luxury houses); Albacete (knives/garden steel); Mallorca (textiles, ikat *llengües*); Galicia (linen, baskets).
- **Specialties:** leather goods (Ubrique = stealth luxury source), ceramics/azulejos, esparto/palm baskets, forged steel, olive-wood, glass.
- **MOQ:** leather 50–200; ceramics 100–300; baskets 100–500. **Pricing:** mid-high leather, mid ceramics. **LT:** 60–120 days.
- **Trade-offs:** leather is premium-priced; some clusters aging out.
- **Hidden opportunity:** **Ubrique private-label leather** (aprons, garden/tool rolls, jewelry trays, dopp kits) at luxury quality without luxury brand cost. **Esparto basketry** in the southeast is undervalued.

### FRANCE
- **Region/City:** Grasse (fragrance capital); Provence (linen, lavender, soap — Marseille/Salon); Vallauris/Aubagne (pottery); Biot (glass); Thiers (knives — Laguiole region); Brittany (linen, salt — fleur de sel).
- **Specialties:** fragrance & naturals, soap, table linen (Libeco-adjacent / Beauvillé), knives, glass, salt, antiques (see Part 6).
- **MOQ:** fragrance via houses (Robertet, Mane, Expressions Parfumées) typically 50–300kg of finished or compound dependent; soap 1,000+; linen 100–300. **Pricing:** high. **LT:** 90–150 days.
- **Trade-offs:** expensive; MOQs for true Grasse naturals are real; "Made in France" is a margin tax you partly recover in story.
- **Hidden opportunity:** **mid-size Grasse houses** (not Givaudan/Firmenich) will develop a proprietary scent at human MOQs — your signature fragrance origin story. **Provençal soap ateliers** for cold-process private label with real provenance.

### ITALY
- **Region/City:** Tuscany/Florence (leather, San Lorenzo; **Santa Maria Novella** apothecary heritage; Impruneta terracotta); Deruta & Montelupo (maiolica); Vietri sul Mare (Amalfi ceramics); Murano/Venice (glass); Como (silk); Puglia (rustic ceramics, olive wood, stone).
- **Specialties:** terracotta (Impruneta = frost-proof garden pots, the best in the world), hand-painted ceramics, leather, glass, silk, apothecary/herbal.
- **MOQ:** terracotta 20–100 (large pieces); ceramics 100–300; leather 50–200. **Pricing:** mid-high to high. **LT:** 90–150 days.
- **Trade-offs:** premium; some maiolica reads "touristy" if not art-directed; family workshops have capacity limits.
- **Hidden opportunity:** **Impruneta garden terracotta** (signed, frost-proof, generational) as a hero antique-adjacent garden line. **Puglia's rustic stoneware** for an exclusive, earthy table line at better margins than Tuscan names.

### TURKEY
- **Region/City:** Buldan & Denizli (loom textiles, peshtemal/Turkish towels); Bursa (silk, textiles); Gaziantep (copper, mother-of-pearl, soap — Nizip olive-oil soap); Kütahya & İznik (ceramics/tile); Cappadocia (pottery).
- **Specialties:** towels/peshtemal, hammam goods, copperware, olive-oil soap, hand-knotted textiles, ceramics.
- **MOQ:** towels 300–1,000; copper 50–200; soap 1,000+. **Pricing:** low-mid (peshtemal FOB ~$3–7). **LT:** 45–90 days.
- **Trade-offs:** quality variance; need strong QC; currency volatility (can be an advantage).
- **Hidden opportunity:** **Buldan loom workshops** for exclusive hand-loomed linens/throws at extraordinary value; **Gaziantep olive-oil soap & copper** for hammam/bath ritual line with deep margins. Turkey is the single best *margin-to-quality* country for textiles + bath.

### MOROCCO
- **Region/City:** Fez (zellige, pottery, leather, brass); Marrakech (rugs, lighting, baskets); Salé/Safi (ceramics); Atlas/Beni Ourain (wool rugs); Sale (raffia/palm baskets).
- **Specialties:** tadelakt-finished ceramics, zellige, brass/lanterns, Beni Ourain & boucherouite rugs, palm/raffia baskets, hand-loomed wool, leather poufs, rosewater/argan.
- **MOQ:** baskets 100–500; rugs low (handmade, one-offs to 50); ceramics 100–300; argan/rosewater 500+. **Pricing:** low-mid (basket FOB $4–12; rug varies hugely). **LT:** 60–120 days; rugs can be longer.
- **Trade-offs:** consistency, ethical-labor diligence essential, logistics from interior cities.
- **Hidden opportunity:** **named-cooperative argan & rosewater** for body care provenance (women's co-ops = story + ESG); **Beni Ourain one-of-a-kind rugs** as the antiques-adjacent floor category; raffia market baskets as a hero garden/gift SKU.

### INDIA
- **Region/City:** Jaipur/Sanganer & Bagru (block-print, hand-block textiles, blue pottery); Moradabad (brass/metal — "Brass City"); Saharanpur (wood carving); Firozabad (glass); Channapatna (lacquered wood toys); Kutch/Bhuj (embroidery, bandhani, bell metal); Khurja (ceramics); Kerala (coir, spices, copper).
- **Specialties:** block-print & natural-dye textiles, brass/metalware, hand-blown glass, carved wood, embroidery, dhurries, beadwork, incense, ceramics.
- **MOQ:** textiles 100–300/print; brass 50–300; glass 200–1,000. **Pricing:** low (block-print napkin FOB $1.5–4; brass candlestick $4–12). **LT:** 75–120 days.
- **Trade-offs:** QC and consistency are the whole game; ethical sourcing/audit critical; long LT.
- **Hidden opportunity:** **Bagru natural-dye (mud-resist) collectives** for a genuinely sustainable, story-rich textile line; **Moradabad brass** for private-label tabletop/garden at keystone-plus margins; **Channapatna lacquerware** for unique gift objects. India is the engine room of margin for textiles/metal/decor.

### BALI (INDONESIA)
- **Region/City:** Ubud & Gianyar (wood carving, stone, textiles); Celuk (silver — *the* artisan silver cluster); Tabanan (ceramics, Gaya-style); Sidemen (ikat/songket weaving).
- **Specialties:** **artisan silver (Celuk = your jewelry source)**, carved teak/suar wood, stoneware ceramics, ata-grass & rattan baskets, ikat, stone.
- **MOQ:** silver 20–100/design; wood 50–200; baskets 100–500; ceramics 100–300. **Pricing:** low-mid (silver labor cheap; ata basket FOB $6–15). **LT:** 75–120 days.
- **Trade-offs:** silver purity verification, monsoon/LT variance, deforestation diligence on wood.
- **Hidden opportunity:** **Celuk silver granulation/handmade jewelry** at a fraction of Western artisan cost — the backbone of Stillwater's silver & symbolic jewelry line with real handcraft. **Ata-grass baskets** (hand-woven, smoke-cured) as iconic, ownable home/garden SKUs.

### VIETNAM
- **Region/City:** Bat Trang (ceramics, near Hanoi); Hoi An (lanterns, tailoring, silk); Mekong (water hyacinth/seagrass baskets); Hanoi craft villages (lacquer, bamboo).
- **Specialties:** ceramics/stoneware, seagrass & water-hyacinth baskets, lacquerware, bamboo, embroidery, silk.
- **MOQ:** ceramics 300–1,000; baskets 200–1,000. **Pricing:** low (seagrass basket FOB $2–6). **LT:** 60–100 days.
- **Trade-offs:** higher MOQs than India/Bali for ceramics; design needs strong direction; quality rising.
- **Hidden opportunity:** **Bat Trang custom-glaze stoneware** as a scalable private-label dinnerware source (better MOQ economics than Europe); **water-hyacinth basketry** as sustainable, high-margin storage/garden heroes.

### THAILAND
- **Region/City:** Chiang Mai & northern villages (celadon ceramics, teak, textiles, silver — hill tribes); Bangkok (benjarong, silk); Lampang (ceramics).
- **Specialties:** celadon, benjarong, mango-wood & teak, hill-tribe silver/textiles, natural soap & spa (Thai wellness supply chain is deep), incense.
- **MOQ:** ceramics 200–500; wood 100–300; spa/soap 500–1,000. **Pricing:** low-mid. **LT:** 60–100 days.

# PART 4 — VENDOR DISCOVERY SYSTEM

Elite buyers don't "find vendors." They run a **continuous intelligence operation**. Here's the repeatable system.

### The discovery channels, ranked by signal quality
1. **Hotel & resort gift shops** (highest signal). Luxury hotels pre-vet artisans you'd never find. Walk the shop at Amangiri, Hotel Emma, Blackberry Farm, any Auberge/Aman property, Soho House — photograph every label, note the maker, contact direct. The hotel did your curation for free.
2. **Museum stores** (MoMA, V&A, Cooper Hewitt, local craft museums) — design-vetted makers, often small.
3. **Artisan guilds & cooperatives** — direct, exclusive, story-rich: ADC (Aid to Artisans), regional craft councils, India's Dastkar, Oaxaca's collectives, Peru's CTTC (Center for Traditional Textiles of Cusco). These bypass middlemen.
4. **Design fairs & maker shows** (curated, not mass): Maison&Objet (Paris — but go for the *small halls*, not the majors), Ambiente (Frankfurt), London Design Festival/Cheltenham, ICFF, Field+Supply (Hudson Valley), American Field, Renegade, Capsule. The brief warns against trade-show commodities — so work the *edges*: the back rows, the first-time exhibitors, the maker markets adjacent to the big fairs.
5. **Antique markets & estate sales** (for antiques + inspiration): Marché aux Puces (Paris/Saint-Ouen), L'Isle-sur-la-Sorgue (Provence), Round Top (Texas), Brimfield (MA), Ardingly/Newark (UK), Arezzo & Parma (Italy). See Part 6.
6. **Independent designers / ateliers** discovered via design press (Cabana, The World of Interiors, Cereal, Kinfolk, Toast Magazine, Frame).
7. **Instagram** (the modern showroom). Method: build a discovery account; follow makers; mine **hashtags by craft + place** (#celukstudio, #naturaldye, #studiopottery, #impruneta); read **who the tastemakers tag**; use "Suggested for you" as a recommendation engine; DM directly. Saved-collections folders by category.
8. **Pinterest** (trend + reverse-image sourcing): reverse-image search a product you love → find the maker; board by category to read aggregate aesthetic direction.
9. **Substack** (the new trade press): maker-economy, slow-living, and design writers profile artisans before they're known. Follow founders who write about their supply chains.
10. **Architecture, landscape & hospitality designers** — partner with the firms designing the hotels/homes your customer wants. They have the maker rolodex and the trust. (AD100/ELLE Decor A-List firms; landscape designers for garden category; hospitality design firms like Roman & Williams, Studio Shamshiri, AvroKO.)
11. **Local maker communities** near your stores/estate — host markets, become the platform, and *graduate* the best locals into your assortment (Magnolia/Terrain model).

### The repeatable system (run this quarterly)
1. **DEFINE THE GAP.** Start from the Category Blueprint (Part 5): "We need a best-tier garden trug and a private-label evening-ritual candle." Discovery is hypothesis-driven, never random.
2. **SCOUT (continuous).** Maintain a living **Vendor Pipeline** (Airtable/Notion) with columns: maker · country/cluster · category · channel found · MOQ · FOB · LT · exclusivity available? · ESG status · sample requested/received · score.
3. **TRIAGE.** Score each lead 1–5 on: aesthetic fit · story/provenance · exclusivity potential · margin · reliability/capacity · ethics. Pursue 4–5s.
4. **SAMPLE.** Always sample before commitment. Test for quality, consistency (order 2–3 of same to read variance), and shipping survival.
5. **PILOT.** Small first PO or **limited-edition drop** as a live market test (Part 2 testing methodology). Read sell-through.
6. **GRADUATE OR CUT.** Winners → core + negotiate **exclusivity/private label**. Losers → cut cleanly.
7. **DEEPEN.** For winners, visit in person, build the relationship, co-develop exclusive product, lock capacity. Relationship = moat.
8. **DOCUMENT THE STORY.** Capture maker name, photos, process, place — this is product-spec content for Part 7/marketing.

> **The buyer's secret:** the best vendors are not looking for you. You find them through *other people's curation* (hotels, museums, designers, guilds), then you offer them what big retailers won't: **exclusivity, story credit, fair terms, and patience.** That's how a small brand out-sources a big one.

# PART 6 — ANTIQUES STRATEGY

*How Bunny Williams, RH, and elite dealers actually buy — and how Stillwater should.*

### How the elites buy
- **Bunny Williams / Treillage model:** the designer's *eye* sources for projects first, retail second. Buys at European markets and from trusted pickers, layers old + new, sells the *look* and the pedigree. Margin via designer authority and one-of-a-kind scarcity. Restoration is light — patina is the product.
- **RH model:** doesn't truly "antique" at scale; it **reproduces** the antique aesthetic (mass production of the look) and supplements with curated vintage. The lesson: *the antique aesthetic can be productized; true antiques are the brand-credibility halo.*
- **Elite dealers (Marché aux Puces, L'Isle-sur-la-Sorgue, Brimfield/Round Top top tier):** buy in volume on multi-week European trips, fill **40-ft containers**, blend high-turn smalls with statement anchors, and price on *story + rarity + restoration*, not cost-plus.

### Sourcing trips & European buying routes
- **The classic loop:** **Paris** (Marché aux Puces de Saint-Ouen — Vernaison/Paul Bert for the eye) → **L'Isle-sur-la-Sorgue, Provence** (the great brocante town; Sunday market + permanent dealers; *the* buying mecca) → **Avignon/Nîmes/Béziers** brocantes → drive north or hit **Belgium** (Tongeren Sunday market — Europe's best antiques flea; Brussels Place du Jeu de Balle) → **England** (Ardingly/IACF, Newark, Kempton, Sunbury, the West Country shops) → optionally **Italy** (Arezzo Fiera Antiquaria — first Sunday; Parma Mercanteinfiera).
- **Timing:** buy in shoulder seasons; arrive early (dawn) at fairs — the trade buys before the public. Build dealer relationships → they hold pieces and call you first (the real edge).
- **Logistics:** consolidate via a regional shipper/consolidator (e.g., a Provence-based packer/forwarder), pack a **20-ft (~$3–6k freight) or 40-ft (~$6–10k) container**, factor breakage, customs, and duty. Antiques (>100 years) often enter the US **duty-free** under HTS 9706 — *huge* margin advantage, but documentation/provenance must support the classification.

### Restoration standards
- **Conserve, don't refinish.** Original patina, paint, and surface = value; stripping destroys it. Acceptable: structural stabilization (re-glue joints, secure veneer), cleaning, food-safe finishing on treen/boards, rewiring lighting to code, new mirror plate only if shattered (keep old glass if possible).
- **Disclose all work.** "Original surface," "later paint," "restored," "married" (parts from two pieces), "associated" — these terms are the trust contract.

### Pricing formulas & markup models
- **Smalls (ironstone, treen, linens, breadboards, demijohns):** **2.5–3.5x landed cost** (keystone-plus; these turn fast and carry the category). Landed = FOB + freight share + duty + restoration.
- **Mid furniture/lighting:** **2.2–2.8x landed.**
- **Statement/rare:** priced on *market comparables and rarity*, not multiple — can be 2x or 5x depending on what the market bears; these sell slowly but anchor perceived value.
- **Rule:** the container must blend to a **~45–55% GM overall**, with smalls subsidizing the slow statement pieces.

### Authentication
- Learn construction tells: hand-cut vs. machine dovetails, saw marks (circular = post-1830s), oxidation/shrinkage, wear consistency (genuine wear is logical, not uniform), patina under/behind, period hardware, secondary woods by region. Buy reference books; apprentice your eye at the markets; build relationships with dealers who guarantee authenticity in writing for high-value pieces.

### Categories with highest demand (BUY THESE)
French/English **breadboards & dough bowls**, **ironstone & creamware**, **hotel/restaurant silver (flatware, cloches)**, **demijohns/glass**, **vintage linen (grain sacks, monogrammed sheets)**, **olive jars & confit pots**, **mirrors (gilt, mercury glass)**, **lighting (sconces, lanterns — rewireable)**, **garden antiques (zinc, urns, planters, stone troughs)**, **architectural salvage (corbels, doors, shutters, finials)**, **primitive American (treen, baskets, stoneware crocks, quilts)**, **paintings (unsigned portraits/landscapes — "ancestor" portraits sell)**.

### Categories to AVOID
- **Brown furniture without strong style** (dark Victorian/Edwardian casegoods — the market has collapsed; slow and low-margin).
- **Fragile high-shipping-risk porcelain** unless high-value.
- **Reproductions sold as antiques** (reputational death).
- **Upholstered antiques** (condition/cleanliness/odor risk, low resale).
- **Anything requiring expensive restoration to be sellable** (negative margin trap).
- **Over-restored pieces** (value destroyed).
- **Massive furniture with low turn and high freight/footprint cost** unless statement-anchor.

| **Santa Maria Novella** | Historic apothecary, herbal/medicinal, single-flower & ancient recipes (Acqua di Rosa, Pot Pourri) | Heritage labels, wax seals, monastic provenance = 600-year authenticity, irreproducible | Florence, own herbal tradition, Tuscan botanicals | High; heritage is the moat |
| **Flamingo Estate** | Ingredient-forward, named-provenance (roses from *this* field), sensual/maximal | Bold color, illustrated, abundant, gift-forward = pleasure/luxury/story | Named farms & growers, estate narrative, artisan production | Very high; story premium, PL-dominant |

### What this teaches Stillwater
- **Fragrance architecture:** build a **signature house accord** (Aesop/Diptyque logic) so the brand is *recognizable across categories* — then express it through a **candle → soap → body → EDP/room ladder** (the candle is the low-risk gateway that recruits customers into higher-margin fragrance).
- **Scent families to build (the Stillwater wardrobe):**
  - *Signature:* a **"stillwater" accord** — clean mineral/water, white tea, fig leaf, soft musk, cedar (calm, genderless, timeless).
  - *Morning:* citrus/herbal (bergamot, rosemary, mint) — energizing ritual.
  - *Evening:* amber/wood/vanilla (sandalwood, tonka, smoke) — winding down.
  - *Sabbath/sacred:* frankincense, myrrh, cedar, beeswax — devotional.
  - *Garden/seasonal:* tomato leaf, lavender, fig, woodsmoke (rotating drops).
- **Packaging psychology for Stillwater:** **refillable apothecary** (sustainability + collectibility + repurchase of refills at higher margin), restrained label with negative space, weight in the vessel, hand-finished cues (Le Labo's dated label), and a **wax-seal/heritage signal** for the sacred line. Vessel should be a *keepable object* (stoneware from Portugal/Vietnam) — the empty vessel becomes a vase/holder, extending brand presence.
- **Ingredient sourcing:** name your provenance (Flamingo lesson) — Calabrian bergamot, Bulgarian rose, Provençal lavender, Indian sandalwood (sustainable/Mysore-alt), beeswax from a named apiary. *Provenance = the premium.*
- **Margin structure:** candles 65–72% (COGS heavy on glass/wax — control vessel cost), soap 70–78%, body care 68–75%, room/linen spray 72–80% (cheap to make, high perceived value — a margin hero), EDP 75–85% (juice is cheap; the *brand* is the cost) — but EDP needs scale/MOQ commitment.

### White-label vs. proprietary fragrance development
- **Phase 1 (launch):** **white-label/private-label** via a quality co-packer (Thailand, Korea, Provence, or a US clean-beauty co-packer) using a **library scent customized** to approximate your house accord. Fast, low MOQ (500–1,000/SKU), low capital. Gets you to market with margin.
- **Phase 2 (scale):** **proprietary fragrance development** with a **mid-size Grasse house** (Robertet, Mane, Expressions Parfumées, or a boutique nose). Brief the perfumer on the "stillwater" accord; develop an exclusive compound you *own*. MOQ on compound is real (often 25–100kg) but unlocks true differentiation and protects the brand from copycats. Your signature scent becomes the irreproducible moat (Diptyque's Baies, Le Labo's Santal 33).
- **The play:** white-label the *secondary* scents forever (margin + flexibility); own the *signature* accord outright (moat + story). Use the signature across candle/body/room so one proprietary compound amortizes across many SKUs.

# PART 8 — HOTEL RETAIL MODEL

*Reverse-engineering Auberge, Blackberry Farm, Hotel Emma, Soho House, Amangiri — and what Stillwater steals.*

### The hotel-retail logic
| Property | Retail logic |
|---|---|
| **Auberge Resorts** | Each property has a curated boutique reflecting *place*; sells the in-room amenities (the bath line you loved at turndown is for sale at checkout); local-artisan + house-brand mix; high gift conversion at departure. |
| **Blackberry Farm** | **The estate IS the product.** Sells its own preserves, spirits, wool (their own sheep), books, larder goods, body care — guests buy a *piece of the experience to take home*. The gold standard of estate-driven retail and the closest model to Stillwater. |
| **Hotel Emma (San Antonio)** | Larder + bookstore + curated shop; literary/historical narrative; sells local + house goods; the shop is a *destination* even for non-guests (community anchor). |
| **Soho House** | Members buy the aesthetic via **Soho Home**; in-house everything; membership = the flywheel; retail extends the lifestyle beyond the visit. |
| **Amangiri** | Minimalist desert luxury; tightly curated, high-price, place-specific objects; scarcity and *taste-credential* purchases; the scent of the property is the memory trigger. |

### What Stillwater steals
1. **Assortment planning = experience-mirroring.** Sell *exactly what the guest touched* — the candle in the room, the robe they wore, the salt in the bath, the dinnerware at breakfast. The experience is a 24-hour product demo. Every touchpoint is a SKU.
2. **Room-to-retail strategy.** Place the signature scent, robe, linens, body care, and tabletop *in use* throughout the experience, with discreet "available in our shop" cards. The **turndown bath ritual** sells the spa line; the **breakfast table** sells home goods.
3. **Signature scent strategy.** One **proprietary property scent** (the "stillwater" accord) diffused throughout = the **memory hook**. Smell is the strongest memory trigger; guests buy the candle/room spray to *re-trigger the feeling at home*. This single tactic drives the highest-margin repeat category. (Aman/Auberge do exactly this.)
4. **Gift strategy.** Departure is peak emotional + peak wallet-open. Stage a **gift wall at checkout**: ready-to-gift ritual sets, "take the experience home" bundles, and "gift it forward" sympathy/celebration sets. Pre-pack so it's frictionless.
5. **Guest conversion strategy.** Capture email at booking; post-stay flow ("relive your stay — the scent, the ritual, the table"); the retreat/experience becomes the top of the funnel that converts to product + membership.
6. **Membership opportunities.** **Stillwater Membership** = the Soho House move adapted: members get retreat access, member pricing, monthly ritual subscription, member-only drops and events, and "founding member" identity. Converts one-time guests into recurring congregation; this is the LTV and moat layer.

> **The synthesis for Stillwater:** even without owning a hotel at launch, **build the brand as if a Stillwater estate/retreat exists** (and partner to host retreats at independent estates / Auberge-type properties — Part 5/9). The *experience* validates the products; the *products* let people take the experience home. Eventually, a flagship store with a café/spa-treatment-room/retreat layer (RH + Terrain + Blackberry logic) closes the loop.

# PART 9 — MERCHANDISING

### Store layout (the journey is a ritual)
- **Threshold/decompression zone (first 15 ft):** scent + sound + low light shift. The signature scent diffused; a water/stillness feature; a single hero vignette (no selling yet — *transition the customer's nervous system*, RH/Aesop logic). This is where "slow" is installed.
- **The path:** a deliberate, slightly meandering route (not a grid) that moves customer through *story zones*, not departments. Decompression → Home/Table → Ritual/Spa → Antiques (the soul/anchor) → Garden → Jewelry/Gifts (near exit/impulse) → checkout/gift wall.
- **The anchor:** **antiques + a hospitality moment** (café/tea bar/treatment room) at the *back* (gravitational pull through the store, RH/Terrain).
- **The exit:** gift wall + jewelry + small consumables at checkout (impulse).

### Visual merchandising principles
- **Vignettes, not shelves.** Sell *scenes* — a set table, a made bed, a drawn bath, a potting bench — so the customer buys the *feeling* and the *whole composition* (raises AOV; solves decision fatigue).
- **Abundance within restraint.** Tonal palettes; group by color/material; negative space around hero/premium pieces (Aesop/Toast); density only in "treasure-hunt" zones (antiques, gifts — Anthropologie/Derian).
- **Height & texture layering.** Mix rough (antique wood, baskets, stone) with refined (silver, glass, linen) — the patina-meets-polish tension that reads "collected, not bought."
- **Real materials, real light.** Warm light (2700K), natural materials on fixtures (reclaimed wood, zinc, stone), living elements (cut branches, herbs, plants — Terrain).

### Adjacencies (the cross-sell logic)
- Candle ↔ matches ↔ tray ↔ book (the "evening" vignette).
- Dinnerware ↔ linens ↔ serving ↔ candle ↔ bottle of estate olive oil (the "table" → buy the whole scene).
- Robe ↔ slippers ↔ bath salt ↔ body oil ↔ towel (the "bath ritual" → the spa-in-a-box cross-sell).
- Seeds ↔ apron ↔ trug ↔ tools ↔ harvest basket (the "garden" scene).
- Jewelry ↔ gift wrap ↔ card (the "I'll just add this" impulse at checkout).

### Storytelling displays
- **Maker cards** at each artisan product (name, place, process, photo) — provenance as merchandising.
- **"Just landed" antique table** with origin notes (scarcity + story).
- **Ingredient theater** for spa (Flamingo logic): show the bergamot, the lavender, the beeswax beside the product.
- **The ritual wall:** the grief/celebration/Sabbath rituals displayed as *complete systems* with the written cards visible — this is the brand's emotional thesis made physical.

### Seasonal transitions
- **4 major + 2 minor resets/year.** Spring (garden/seeds/fresh), Summer (outdoor entertaining/garden harvest), Autumn (harvest/Sabbath/gathering), Winter/Holiday (gifts/rituals/candles — the Q4 engine), plus minor New Year (intention/ritual) and Easter/spring-faith moment.
- **Scent-led transitions:** change the diffused store scent seasonally → cues newness, drives candle drops.
- **Hold the timeless core; rotate the seasonal edge** (Toast discipline, not Anthropologie churn).

### Impulse zones
- **Checkout:** small candles, soaps, lip/hand balm, single jewelry pieces, cards, $18–45 "secretly buy" items (Part 10).
- **Gift wall:** pre-packed sets at 3 price points.
- **The "one more thing" basket** beside the register (matches, sachets, seed packets, single chocolates/larder smalls).

### Hospitality moments
- **A drink on arrival** (tea/infused water — the decompression).
- **A treatment/sampling bar** (try the body oil; smell the scent wardrobe).
- **A café/tea room** as dwell-anchor (Terrain/Blackberry).
- **Workshops & events** in-store (turns the store into the *experience product* — Part 5).
- **Hand-wrapping at checkout** (the unboxing begins in store; theater + value).

## CLOSING — The founder's mandate

Stillwater wins not by being a better home store, but by owning a **feeling no competitor has claimed: the consecration of ordinary time.** The strategy is therefore:

1. **Lead with ritual** (the moat no one else has) — especially grief, Sabbath, and hospitality.
2. **Fund the brand with private-label consumables** (spa/ritual at 65–75% GM, replenishable, refillable).
3. **Build credibility with antiques and best-tier artisan goods** (Japan, Italy, the curated container).
4. **Earn margin and exclusivity through deep, fair artisan relationships** discovered via hotels, guilds, and designers — not trade-show commodities.
5. **Sell the whole scene, not the SKU** (vignette merchandising, the complete table, the bath ritual).
6. **Convert customers into congregation** via membership, subscription, and retreats (the LTV engine).
7. **Make scent the memory hook** — one owned accord across every category.
8. **Operate like an estate** even before owning one — the experience validates the product; the product lets people take the experience home.

*Build the brand a discerning, time-starved woman would choose to make permanent in her life — and then give her a reason to belong, not just to buy.*
